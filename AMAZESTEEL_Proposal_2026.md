## 1. Excellence

### 1.1 Quality and pertinence of the project's research and innovation objectives

Traditional moment-resisting connections (MRCs) in steel structures are heavy, rigid, hard to reuse, and often the "Achilles heel" in seismic events — as seen recently in the Colombia, Japan, Italy and Mexico earthquakes. These issues weaken the safety, sustainability, and adaptability of modern steel structures.

To meet these challenges, **AMAZESTEEL** utilizes Additive Manufacturing (AM) — specifically **Wire-Arc AM (WAAM)** combined with **structural topology optimization (STO)** tools — to develop novel, lightweight, durable, and recyclable MRCs with seismic resilience. The project aligns with the European Green Deal, Horizon Europe Cluster 4 (Digital, Industry & Space), the Just Transition Mechanism, and Circular Economy Action Plan policies, enabling lower-carbon, reusable MRCs and cutting into the buildings sector's 21% share of global greenhouse-gas emissions.

**Snapshot — Today vs. AMAZESTEEL:**

| | Traditional MRCs | AMAZESTEEL WAAM–STO MRCs |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Baseline | ≥30% lighter |
| Material | — | ER70S-6 wire, WAAM-printed |
| Standards | EN 1993 | EN 1993 verified |
| End of life | — | Recyclable |
| Gap | No AM-MRC seismically qualified | Precise, seismically tested |

**Testing programme:** Five specimens (S1–S5) on one testing rig — S1–S2 monotonic, S3–S5 cyclic.

**Vision → Objective → Impact:**

| Vision | Objective | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Advance seismic resilience with WAAM-STO MRC for a circular, low-carbon built environment. | Design, fabricate and validate full-scale WAAM-STO joints against Eurocode prequalification criteria. | Target performance: ≥30% lower CO₂, ≥10% lower cost/joint, ≥0.04 rad at 0.80·Mp. |

AMAZESTEEL defines four SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) Research Objectives (RO1–RO4):

**Table 1 — Research Objectives and corresponding Work Packages (WPs)**

- **❖ RO1 — Select, model, redesign, and fabricate representative MRCs (FE + STO + WAAM) — WP3**
  A recent publication showed AM-fabricated MRCs can reach 10% higher strength with a 35% weight reduction (1.7× strength per unit mass), but that study was limited to partial-strength, component-scale specimens under static load; FE models of AM-MRCs ignored nonlinearities and Eurocode-based imperfections; and current STO workflows rarely encode WAAM constraints (bead width, build direction, overhang), leading to impractical designs. AMAZESTEEL will integrate a Eurocode-compliant FE model with STO explicitly constrained by WAAM feasibility rules to generate full-scale MRCs. **Hypothesis:** WAAM-constrained STO cuts connection-zone mass by at least 30% while matching the EJ+ reference's moment resistance and satisfying EC-3 imperfection checks.

- **❖ RO2 — Assess seismic performance of WAAM-STO MRCs under cyclic loading — WP3 & WP4**
  Recent WAAM connections have been examined only monotonically; no study has tested their cyclic or seismic response, and no AM joint is pre-qualified in EN 1998-1-2 Annex E. AMAZESTEEL will test WAAM-STO MRCs under the AISC 341-16 quasi-static cyclic protocol used in EQUALJOINTS+ (EJ+, EU project), deriving fragility and risk metrics via incremental dynamic analysis (IDA). **Hypothesis:** ~25% higher cumulative energy dissipation, confirmed or refuted by specimens S4–S5 against the traditional reference S3. The central open question is whether they meet prequalification criteria under EC-8 ductility class DC3 and AISC 341 — resolving it delivers the first EC-8-referenced qualification evidence for AM-MRC.

- **❖ RO3 — Benchmark WAAM-STO MRCs against traditional welded/bolted MRCs — WP4**
  Traditional MRCs are thoroughly tested for load capacity, ductility and rotational capacity, underpinning EC-3, but no systematic comparison evaluates WAAM-STO MRCs against these benchmarks. AMAZESTEEL provides the first benchmarking framework, comparing structural, manufacturing, economic (cost/joint), and reliability criteria against the published EJ+ dataset. **Hypothesis:** WAAM-STO MRCs achieve at least 10% lower cost per joint while providing a reliability dataset supporting EC-3 design guidance.

- **❖ RO4 — Evaluate sustainability of WAAM-STO MRCs through LCA and LCC frameworks — WP5**
  AM-related Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) is currently limited to structural members and gridshell joints under static design; none couples environmental and economic assessment for an MRC under seismic demand. AMAZESTEEL provides the first LCA framework for WAAM-STO MRCs, combined with Life Cycle Costing (LCC) under seismic demand. Scope covers cradle-to-gate and cradle-to-grave; key indicators are Global Warming Potential (GWP, CO₂ eq), circularity/recyclability, and embodied CO₂, normalized to a 0–1 scale. The LCC converts RO2 fragility results into expected seismic repair losses — the first connection-scale assessment driven by seismic fragility. **Hypothesis:** WAAM-STO MRCs reduce life-cycle CO₂ by ≥30% while improving reuse and recyclability.

### Urgency and Prior Work

As AM becomes more popular in steel construction due to its speed and accuracy, the lack of codified, seismic-qualified AM-MRCs is a critical gap. Although AM-MRCs show promising static performance, their behavior under cyclic and seismic loads remains untested, and EC-8-compliant designs have not yet been investigated.

- **Prior EU projects:** LASIMM (Horizon 723600) advanced AM hardware; CONSTRUCTADD (RFCS 101057957) targeted EN 1090/EC-3 qualification of AM steel parts — neither addressed seismic behavior.
- Traditional MRCs were tested monotonically and cyclically in **EQUALJOINTS** and **EQUALJOINTS+ "EJ+"** (RFCS-754048), and in the damage-controlled **FREEDAM** (RFSR-CT-2015-00022); steel reuse frameworks for embodied-carbon savings appeared in **PROGRESS** (RFCS-747847). None combines AM, STO, cyclic loading, and connection-scale sustainability — AMAZESTEEL delivers that combination.

**Timing:** EN 1998-1-2:2026 is published; national annexes are in preparation until March 2028; clause E.3.3.14 defines the standing route by which new joint typologies enter pre-qualification. Supervisor Prof. B. Gil sits on **CEN/TC 250/SC3/WG8**, giving a direct route to present M18–M23 outputs there.

#### RO1 methodology — Select, model, redesign, fabricate (FE + STO + WAAM)

- **Select:** A prequalified MRC — an extended unstiffened end-plate joint (IPE360–HEB280, S355), listed in EN 1998-1-2:2026 Annex E — is chosen from the EJ+ project as reference, since its hysteretic curves are already published.
- **Model:** The MRC is modeled in ABAQUS using the full parameter set characterizing MRC behavior (stiffness, resistance, rotation capacity) per EN 1993-1-8, with geometric imperfections following the EJ+ assumption of a buckling-mode shape at 80% of EN 10034 mill tolerances. Validation requires matching the FE model's output to the published EJ+ curves within 10% on initial stiffness, resistance, and rotation capacity.
- **Redesign:** The validated FE model is regenerated by STO using a density-based SIMP (Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization) approach in ABAQUS/Tosca to minimize connection-zone mass, while meeting EN 1993-1-8 stiffness/resistance requirements for the EJ+ reference. WAAM feasibility constraints enter as geometric limits:
  - Minimum member size ≈ 6 mm (twice the bead width)
  - Maximum overhang angle of 45°
  - Fixed build direction
  - Ductility constraint ensuring the dissipative region has minimum thickness
- **Fabricate:** The same FE framework verifies stiffness, resistance, and rotation capacity of the optimized WAAM-STO geometry against the same EN 1993-1-8 checks as the EJ+ prequalified MRC before any prototype is printed. Specimens are fabricated on the **Addilan WAAM cell at Universidad Pública de Navarra (UPNA)** in ER70S-6 carbon steel, under Prof. Fernando Veiga (UPNA), in collaboration with the **Automated Analysis and Design of Structures (AADS)** group at University of Navarra (UNAV). Monotonic and cyclic test coupons in three build orientations (ISO 6892, ISO 12106) supply anisotropic material cards for the FE models and support derivation of randomness/hardening factors for WAAM steel. Each specimen is characterized for dimensional accuracy, residual stresses, and microstructure, updating the FE model in an iterative **FE-STO-WAAM loop** (D3.2, WP3).

**Experimental campaign** at **University of Oviedo (UNIOVI)**, Spain, in two secondment cycles, comprising five full-scale test specimens (S1–S5) plus one optional specimen held in reserve:
- **First cycle (M11–M12):** two monotonic tests — one traditional MRC (S1), validated against published EJ+ curves, and one WAAM-STO prototype (S2), validating the FE-STO-WAAM chain. Monotonic loading follows the EJ+ guideline, with unloading-reloading branches at 0.02 and 0.03 rad to recover initial stiffness.
- UNIOVI is used for the full campaign because the IPE360-HEB280 assembly, tested in prequalification configuration, exceeds the 2175 mm clearance of the UNAV testing hall; UNIOVI's multipositional reaction frame and 750 kN IBERTEST actuator accommodate full-scale specimens.
- Only the STO-MRC zone is printed; beam and column members remain conventional hot-rolled sections, reused where post-test inspection permits and replaced where the balanced web panel (a dissipative component of the EJ+ reference) has yielded. Connection detailing follows the EJ+ reference, so only the printed zone differs.
- **Success check:** AM prototypes pass EC-3 checks; static capacity within ±10% of FE, verified at M12 with the S1–S2 monotonic tests.

#### RO2 methodology — Seismic performance under cyclic loading (FE + quasi-static cyclic + IDA)

FE models from RO1 are extended for dynamic assessment, with Chaboche combined isotropic-kinematic hardening on the cyclic test coupons and ductile damage via a cyclic void growth model.

- **Second secondment cycle (M14–M16):** three quasi-static cyclic tests at UNIOVI — one traditional reference (S3) and two nominally identical WAAM-STO specimens (S4, S5), giving repeatability against WAAM process variability. Higher dissipation at lower mass is hypothesized to come from a more uniformly yielding optimized volume, not added material.
- **Protocol:** AISC 341-16, matching the EJ+ prequalification tests on the IPE360-HEB280 assembly for direct comparability. The protocol imposes symmetric drift cycles: 6 cycles at each amplitude up to 0.75%, 4 cycles at 1%, then 2 cycles at each increasing amplitude, continued to failure. The EJ+ protocol (calibrated to European ground motions) produces comparable cumulative demand at 0.04 rad as a secondary check.
- **Acceptance criteria** (either): EN 1998-1-2 requires plastic rotation ≥0.03 rad in DC3, evaluated on the first-cycle envelope; or AISC 341 requires ≥80% of beam plastic moment retained at 0.04 rad story drift. Since no AM joint is pre-qualified, qualification follows the **EN 1998-1-2 E.3.3.14** route for joint types not listed in Annex E.
- **Seismic hazard:** taken from ESHM20 via OpenQuake at L'Aquila (42.34°N, 13.40°E), Italy, with 22 records matched to the conditional spectrum at T₁.
- Calibrated MRC models are embedded in a numerical four-story, three-bay DC3 frame archetype (6 m spans, 3.5 m story heights), consistent with EJ+ prequalification validity.
- **Nonlinear Time-History Analysis (NTHA)** at design intensity verifies response against code limits; **IDA** on the same archetype produces fragility curves with joint plastic rotation as demand parameter, Sa(T₁) as intensity measure, and damage states at the DC3 0.03 rad limit and at 0.80·Mp.
- **Central research gap:** whether WAAM-STO MRCs satisfy EC-8 dissipative-zone ductility requirements — delivered via D4.1 (WP4).
- **Success check:** characterized cyclic hysteretic behavior and a validated open fragility dataset for the WAAM-STO MRC.

#### RO3 methodology — Benchmark WAAM-STO MRCs against traditional joints (Performance-Cost-Reliability)

Quantifies WAAM-STO vs. traditional MRC using specimens created in RO1 and tested in RO2 at UNIOVI, under an identical member, frame, and loading protocol, so the comparison isolates the combined WAAM-plus-STO effect (with the coupon set separating the material contribution). Traditional specimens (S1, S3) are first validated against published EJ+ curves to establish a reference.

- **Comparison spans:** structural performance (capacity, ductility, rotation capacity), manufacturing (dimensional accuracy, throughput, rework), and production economics (€/joint — built from the same primary fabrication data as the WP5 inventory: wire, shielding gas, energy, machining, and rework), compared against the EJ+ reference.
- **Scoring:** each indicator scores 1 for x ≥ 1, falls linearly to 0 at x = 0.5 (where x = specimen value ÷ EJ+ reference value), combined in a weighted-sum score with weights: structural 0.5, economic 0.3, manufacturing 0.2 (so ductility and capacity dominate and cannot be offset by cost alone); weights varied ±0.1 as sensitivity check.
- A WAAM-STO MRC scores 1 on structural efficiency if it matches/exceeds reference capacity per unit weight; 1 on ductility if it meets/exceeds reference ductility; 1 on rotation capacity if it reaches ≥0.04 rad drift while retaining 0.80·Mp (the EJ+ acceptance threshold); 1 on economy if cost per joint is ≥10% lower.
- **(D4.2, WP4) Success check:** Open performance-cost-reliability database — WAAM-STO & traditional MRCs.

#### RO4 methodology — Sustainability via LCA and LCC (ISO 14040/44 & Circularity metrics)

LCA and LCC executed per ISO 14040/44 and EN 15804. LCA covers **cradle-to-gate** (raw materials to production, mandatory baseline) and **cradle-to-grave** (use, maintenance, reuse, recycling, disposal).

- **Life-cycle inventory:** primary fabrication data from ER70S-6 carbon-steel deposition at UPNA (wire mass, shielding gas, energy, machining, rejected material, transport) combined with Ecoinvent and SimaPro secondary data, supervised by Prof. J. Torres Ramo & Prof. P. González Martínez (UNAV).
- **Functional unit:** one beam-to-column connection delivering the EJ+ reference moment capacity over a 50-year service life.
- **Environmental indicators:** GWP (CO₂ eq/MRC), embodied carbon, recyclability, circularity, with sensitivity analysis on deposition rate, energy mix, and shielding gas.
- **LCC:** a 2-month secondment (M21–M22) at **Istituto Universitario di Studi Superiori Pavia, Italy (IUSS)**, under Prof. Roberto Nascimbene. The LCC carries the RO3 production cost forward, combining it with maintenance and expected seismic repair losses derived from converting WP4 fragility functions into expected losses over a 50-year reference service life (EN 1990), following the FEMA P-58 framework, with repair consequence functions built from T4.3 cost/joint data and the ESHM20 hazard curve.
- **(D5.3, WP5) Success check:** connection-scale LCA/LCC with ≥30% life-cycle GWP cut in at least one WAAM-STO MRC variant.

### Interdisciplinarity, Cross-sectorality, and Gender Dimension

AMAZESTEEL is highly interdisciplinary by construction, combining structural/civil engineering, materials science, manufacturing, computational optimization, seismic hazard analysis, and sustainability assessment. It integrates Eurocode-based seismic design with WAAM, STO, and digital fabrication, incorporating data-science methods including experimentally updated FE models and fragility datasets. FE-Modeling acts as a central link — not just an analytical tool but the backbone uniting manufacturing constraints, microstructural test data, seismic hazard inputs, and sustainability indicators into a cohesive design-to-validation cycle.

The project is cross-sectoral by design: the four academic organizations (UNAV, UPNA, UNIOVI, IUSS) are complemented by non-academic partners engaged without contractual status (AM fabricators and design firms) in the two WP6 workshops, plus the ECCS and CEN/TC 250 committees reached via D6.3 and Prof. Gil's committee seats. It is transdisciplinary in that acceptance criteria are set with those committees, not only within academia.

**Gender dimension:** AMAZESTEEL's research on steel connection mechanical behavior has no direct sex/gender-based analytical dimension — this was assessed rather than overlooked (per Horizon Europe guidance). The gender perspective is integrated into how research is carried out: gender-balanced co-supervision of BSc/MSc students in WP2, gender-balanced speaker selection in WP6 workshops, and outreach encouraging young women into structural engineering and AM (dedicated sessions at STEM fairs and EU Researchers' Night), under UNAV's Equality Plan and its successor, monitored through WP6 KPIs.

## 8. Environmental Considerations in Light of the MSCA Green Charter

**Sustainable Implementation Actions:**

- **Green Meetings & Events:** Prioritize virtual tools (teleconferencing, shared digital workspaces) to reduce travel. In-person gatherings held at venues accessible via low-emission transportation, with green catering, paperless handouts, and adherence to EU "green events" guidelines.
- **Eco-Responsible Mobility:** Secondment and dissemination travel favors trains or low-emission transportation where possible; accommodation follows EU Ecolabel or EMAS standards. Intercontinental travel limited to the two conferences where the target community is not reachable in Europe (SSRC/NASCC, the AISC 341 constituency; 19WCEE), with European venues used for all other dissemination and remote participation preferred where the format allows.
- **Green Procurement:** Goods and services comply with EU Green Public Procurement criteria; experimental materials sourced locally to minimize transport-related emissions.

**Integration into Research & Work Packages:**

- **WP3–WP4 (Research & Validation):** Prototype fabrication uses ER70S-6 carbon-steel wire deposited at UPNA in Pamplona, so feedstock is sourced and processed regionally with minimal transport emissions; carbon steel is fully recyclable at end of life. Beam and column steelwork is reused across the test series where post-test inspection permits, and undamaged sections are recycled thereafter. STO itself cuts the steel demand of each printed connection zone. Experimentally updated FE models (the FE-STO-WAAM loop) reduce the number of physical prototypes required, limiting material waste.
- **WP5 (Sustainability Assessment):** Includes Life Cycle Assessment and Life Cycle Costing to measure CO₂ reductions, recyclability, and resource efficiency of additive manufacturing versus welded/bolted joints.
- **WP2 (Training & Knowledge Exchange):** Training sessions cover green research practices, carbon footprint tracking, and sustainable engineering design.
- **Resource & Energy Efficiency:** Facilities use renewable energy sources where available and enforce reduce-reuse-recycle principles. Team members track personal project footprints with EU-approved tools (e.g., Consumer Footprint Calculator) and report reductions during annual reviews.
- **WP6 (Dissemination):** Prioritizes reducing printed material, favors open-access repositories, and adopts digital strategies to lessen environmental impact.
